Live Web Terminal: https://parakh-three.vercel.app  |  Built for: Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food & Public Distribution

What Problem Did We Tackle?

Most packaged goods in Indian stores violate labeling laws without buyers noticing. Brands hide price hikes by quietly cutting packet weight from 100g to 85g without printing the required Unit Sale Price, print mandatory warnings in tiny unreadable text under 1mm, or leave out the phrase "inclusive of all taxes". When government inspectors visit shops, measuring packet text by hand with plastic rulers takes 15 to 20 minutes per item, and photos taken on normal phones get thrown out of court because defense lawyers argue the image could have been altered.

What Did We Build?

We built PARAKH, a fast web application that inspectors can open on any phone or laptop browser without installing any app. The officer snaps photos of the front, back, and sides of a package. Our system uses Google Gemini Flash-Lite to extract the text in under 3 seconds, checks it against 40 legal rules we coded from the official gazette, measures letter heights using an ordinary ₹5 coin as a physical size reference, and seals the photo with a SHA-256 hash and GPS coordinates so the evidence is legally solid in court under Section 63 BSA.

We chose not to build a heavy mobile app that requires Play Store approvals or device-specific APKs. Field officers carry different phones, tablets, or district office laptops. PARAKH runs directly inside modern web browsers using standard browser APIs (HTML5 camera, GPS, and local IndexedDB storage). Any officer can open the URL and start inspecting immediately.

The Real Problem on Indian Retail Shelves

India's Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules, 2011 exist to protect buyers from unfair trade practices. But when we studied how enforcement happens on the ground, we found four major bottlenecks that prevent officers from doing effective inspections.

1. Measuring Letter Heights by Hand Takes Too Long

The law says numbers on labels must be at least 1mm, 2mm, or 4mm tall depending on pack weight. Today, an inspector has to physically hold a plastic ruler or handheld vernier caliper against round bottles, flexible bags, and shiny packets. Checking just one product takes 15 to 20 minutes, meaning an officer can only check a tiny fraction of the items in a store.

2. Shrinkflation Slips Past Unchecked

When raw material costs go up, companies often don't raise the packet price. Instead, they quietly drop a 200g snack packet to 180g while keeping the exact same bag size and ₹50 price tag. Under Rule 6(11), they are required to print the Unit Sale Price (like ₹0.28 per gram), but many skip it or print it incorrectly, leaving consumers unaware of the real price increase.

3. Normal Photos Get Dismissed in Court

When officers take photos of non-compliant products using standard phone cameras, defense lawyers frequently get the cases dismissed under Section 63 of the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023 (formerly Section 65B of the Evidence Act). Without a cryptographic hash, clear timestamp, and device record taken at capture time, there is no technical proof the photo was not edited.

4. Repeat Offenders Hide Across District Borders

Section 36(2) of the Act imposes strict fines and even jail time for repeat corporate offenders. But because district offices keep inspection records in separate paper files, a company caught violating rules in one district can continue doing the same thing in the next town without anyone connecting the dots.

How Our Web App Solves These Challenges

We designed PARAKH as a practical tool that fits right into an inspector's normal field routine without adding extra work.

Our Practical Engineering Approach

  • Quick Multi-Side Capture: The officer opens our website on their phone, snaps the front, back, and sides. Our code resizes the images on the phone to 900px (~180 KB total), so it uploads instantly even on weak 3G/4G shop signals.
  • Deterministic Rules (No AI Guessing): We let AI read the text, but we wrote all 40 legal rules in strict TypeScript code. If a rule says 500g requires 4.0mm height, our code checks the math directly—it never lets an AI model hallucinate whether something is legal or illegal.
  • Using an Ordinary Coin as a Scale: Instead of asking the government to buy ₹15,000 laser tools, the officer simply places a standard ₹5 coin next to the box. Our software knows the coin is 23.0 mm and uses it to measure the text on screen down to 0.1 mm.
  • Automatic Legal Proof: The second a photo arrives, our server calculates its SHA-256 hash, attaches GPS coordinates and atomic time, and locks it with a digital signature so it is legally rock-solid in court.

Key Features We Implemented in Code

1. 40 Legal Rules in Code

We implemented rules PCR-001 through PCR-040 covering manufacturer address, PIN code, standard metric units, MRP taxes phrase, expiry dates, and importer identity.

2. Fast Multimodal AI Vision

We use Google Gemini Flash-Lite to pull label text and bounding boxes from all package sides in under 3 seconds, with local Tesseract.js as an offline backup.

3. On-Screen Coin Caliper

A dual-axis reticle (Caliper X & Y) that lets the officer line up caliper jaws on screen to measure printed numeral heights in real millimeters against a ₹5 coin.

4. Section 63 BSA Evidence Sealer

Our backend calculates the SHA-256 byte hash of the raw photo, binds live GPS coordinates, and creates an HMAC signature with an indelible verification code.

5. Auto-Drafted Form V Notice

Generates official Show-Cause Notices under Section 36(1) and automatically calculates out-of-court compounding fees under Section 48 and the Sixth Schedule.

6. Live GIS Violation Map

An interactive Leaflet map that plots every audit location with color pins (Green for Pass, Red for Fail) and filters by violation type.

7. Shrinkflation / USP Checker

Calculates the mandatory Unit Sale Price (per 1g, 100g, 1ml, or item) and compares it with the printed label to spot arithmetic errors and hidden price rises.

8. Side-by-Side Batch Comparator

Lets an officer pick two past scans (like an old batch vs a new batch) to highlight changed weights, altered price stickers, or deleted declarations.

9. Officer Review & Override

If a label is crumpled or lighting is poor, the review page highlights low-confidence fields so the inspector can quickly fix misread text before saving.

What Makes Our Solution Different? (Real Innovations)

We didn't just build a wrapper around a chatbot. We solved four specific, real-world engineering challenges that field officers deal with every day:

1. Calibrating Scale with an Indian ₹5 Coin

Phone cameras have no idea how big things are in real life without depth sensors. Instead of expecting the government to buy ₹15,000 laser tools for every inspector, we used the fact that every standard Indian ₹5 circulation coin has an exact diameter of 23.0 mm. By placing the coin next to the box, our code calculates pixelsPerMm = coinPixels / 23.0, letting our digital caliper measure text down to 0.1 mm.

2. Building Evidence for India's New Criminal Law

Most student projects stop at displaying a verdict on screen. We looked into the legal requirements of Section 63 of the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023. Our code hashes the raw image bytes using SHA-256 the moment it arrives, locks in the GPS coordinates and atomic time, and signs it with an HMAC secret so defense lawyers cannot claim the photo was tampered with.

3. Bundling All Package Sides Into One AI Call

Products have brand names on the front and manufacturer details on the back. When we first tested sending separate requests for each side, it took 20 seconds. We optimized our pipeline to pack the front, back, and side into a single multimodal prompt. This dropped the latency to ~2.4 seconds and let the AI connect the brand name on the front with the corporate address on the back.

4. Keeping AI Out of Legal Judgments

Large language models are prone to hallucination. We strictly limit the AI to reading the text and identifying box coordinates. All 40 legal rules are coded in deterministic TypeScript. If the law says text on a 500g box must be at least 4.0mm, our code evaluates measuredHeight >= 4.0. The AI never gets to guess if a product complies.

How We Secured the System and Evidence

Because this tool handles legal evidence and government notices, we implemented proper security practices:

Security Area How We Implemented It in Code Why It Matters
Password Hashing Node's native crypto.scrypt with a 16-byte random salt and 64-byte key. Protects passwords against brute force and GPU rainbow tables.
Timing Attacks Verified using crypto.timingSafeEqual when checking passwords and tokens. Prevents attackers from guessing passwords by measuring response times.
Session Management Custom HMAC-SHA256 signed tokens stored in HTTP-only, secure browser cookies. Prevents cookie tampering and stops cross-site scripting (XSS) token theft.
Database Security Parameterized SQL queries throughout (db.prepare('... WHERE id = ?')). Completely prevents SQL injection attacks on both SQLite and Supabase.
Evidence Integrity SHA-256 byte hashing of raw image buffers, bound to GPS coordinates and signed. Proves in court that the photo was not edited after being taken.
In-Memory Processing Images are resized in RAM buffers using Sharp and never left as plain files on disk. Leaves zero temporary image residue on shared serverless containers.
Honest Note on Demonstration Mode: For production, sessions require a secret key (AUTH_SECRET). For local testing and SIH jury evaluation, our code includes an authorized default Field Officer user so judges can test the app immediately without getting blocked by login setup.
